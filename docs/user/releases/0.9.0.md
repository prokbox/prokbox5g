ProkBox5g 0.9.0 is about sound and languages. Every format now plays through the 24-bit output, the equaliser keeps its loudness and has three settings of your own, and a new Settings › Audiophile menu offers optional 88.2 and 96 kHz output. The menus come in German, Dutch and Japanese as well as English, and song, artist and album names in Japanese, Korean and Chinese show correctly and sort the way you expect. Songs the player can't play are greyed out instead of failing silently. The rest is fixes: for pausing, changes of sample rate between songs, short drop-outs in demanding files and more. Some of these fixes have been proven in the simulator and an emulator but not yet on a real player; those are worded with "should" below. Your music, settings, places in books and videos, and playlists are kept when you update.

## What is new

- **Menus in German, Dutch and Japanese.** Choose a language in **Settings › Language**; it changes straight away. Dates, times and numbers follow the language you choose, and long translations wrap instead of being cut off. The texts are drafts that native speakers have not yet checked; [Translations](../translations.md) explains how to send a better word.
- **Japanese, Korean and Chinese names.** Song, artist and album names in Japanese (kana and kanji), Korean (Hangul) and the common Simplified and Traditional Chinese characters now show correctly, and lists sort them in a sensible order. Rarer Chinese characters still show as a blank box.
- **Lists follow your sort tags.** When your files carry sort names, such as Sort Artist or Sort Album, the library sorts by them, so "The Beatles" can sit under B.
- **All music through the 24-bit output.** MP3, AAC and other compressed files now keep the extra detail their decoders produce, instead of being cut to 16 bits. When one song ends and the next has a different sample rate, the player changes over with at most a very short pause.
- **32-bit FLAC files play.** They used to be skipped. Those up to 48 kHz now play, reduced to the player's 24-bit output with a little added noise to keep the quiet parts clean.
- **Your own equaliser settings.** Next to the built-in presets there are three settings of your own, Custom 1 to 3, each with a picture of its curve that you shape with the wheel. Turning on a preset no longer makes everything quieter.
- **Settings › Audiophile.** A new menu with optional 88.2 and 96 kHz output (**Hi-Res**) and a **Stereo Width** control. Hi-Res is off by default: it runs the audio chip faster than it is rated for, so it asks first and tests your player before it turns on. **Hi-Res 128x** goes one step further at 88.2 kHz and up, again after its own test. While music plays at 88.2 kHz or higher, the equaliser presets pause.
- **Format details in Now Playing.** One more press of the centre button shows the song's format, bit depth and sample rate, and what the player is sending to the headphones.
- **Long names scroll in Now Playing.** The artist and album lines now scroll like the title, one line at a time.
- **Songs the player can't play are greyed out.** They stay in your library with a small sign, are skipped during playback, and pressing the centre button on one says why: for example, that the file is copy-protected or in a format the player doesn't read. This works in songs, audiobooks, podcast episodes, Search and On-The-Go.
- **Opus files level their loudness.** Sound Check now uses the loudness tags in Opus files, and Opus tracks end exactly where they should.
- **Better tag reading.** Every genre number in older MP3 tags now has its name, and a song with several artists lists them all while sorting under the first.
- **Video sound on the second processor.** A video's soundtrack, and the click of the wheel, are now made on the second processor, like music, which leaves the first one free for the picture and the menus.
- **Shuffle Songs starts sooner.** Playback begins once the first few songs are ready, and the rest are added behind it. Shuffle order is also fairer in small libraries.

## What is fixed

- **Pause is silent at once.** On some players, sound could carry on for several seconds after you pressed Pause. The sound now stops straight away, even while the next song is loading.
- **The next song after a change of sample rate should play at the right speed.** After a seamless change between, say, a 48 kHz and a 96 kHz song, the next song could play too fast or too slow. A stop at the wrong moment could also leave the player waiting for a change that never came. Both are fixed; the test player has played them correctly on the road.
- **Fewer short drop-outs in demanding files.** High-quality FLAC, 32-bit and Opus files could, rarely, drop out for a moment while the second processor tidied its memory. That work now waits for a safe moment. We have not yet confirmed the fix on a real player.
- **The audio chip runs at its intended voltage.** Like the original software, ProkBox5g now powers the audio chip at 3.0 V instead of 2.5 V, which its maker says gives a little more headroom and a little less noise. We have not measured the difference on a real player.
- **A stricter test for Hi-Res 128x.** The test now plays a few seconds at each rate, including a near-silent stretch, and fails on the smallest break, so a player that would crackle at 128x should not pass.
- **Hi-Res 128x without the faint crackle.** With Hi-Res 128x on, music at 88.2 and 96 kHz now runs the audio chip's sound processing at full speed. At half speed, a very quiet ending could carry a faint crackle; on our test player, full speed played it clean. Hi-Res 128x is still off until you turn it on. Your own equaliser settings and Stereo Width keep playing at these rates, as before; on our test player they played clean there too.
- **The equaliser has a safety net.** While a preset or one of your own settings is on, the audio chip's limiter now gently holds back sudden peaks that would otherwise clip. It was tried by ear on our test player and left the music clean.
- **Manual skips change the sample rate again.** Skipping to a song with a different sample rate now switches the output to suit it.
- **The library survives an update.** The library format changed in 0.9.0. The first start after the update rebuilds the library behind the library page, with a progress bar, instead of showing an empty or muddled library.
- **A rare video-chip hiccup is fixed.** Switching between album art and the visualiser could, once in a long while, upset the video chip. It no longer does.
- **Fewer freezes around album art.** A library update and the album art pass could hold each other up, and in a few cases Play would wait for the art. They now give way to each other.
- **Damaged music files no longer crash the player.** FLAC, WAV, AIFF, M4A, Apple Lossless and game-music (VGM) files with damaged or unusual headers, tags or pictures are now handled safely: the player skips the bad part, or the file, instead of freezing or restarting.
- **A FLAC song with a damaged spot plays on.** It skips past the bad spot instead of stopping the song.
- **24-bit Apple Lossless files keep their full precision.** Before, their lowest 8 bits were lost.
- **More M4A and M4B files play.** Files made by ffmpeg with a very large audio section, and files recorded on Android phones, now play, and seeking in audiobooks longer than 27 hours lands in the right place.
- **The right cover shows.** FLAC songs with several pictures inside show the front cover, a FLAC song with a damaged picture plays without it instead of being skipped, and covers in MP3 files tagged by some Windows tools now show.
- **Better tags from AIFF and game music.** AIFF files show tags stored before the audio, and game-music files with long Japanese titles show the right game and composer.
- **Damaged cover pictures are refused safely.** A few kinds of broken JPEG pictures, including one cut short inside an Ogg file, could make the picture decoder spin. They are now rejected.
- **MP3 files with a skipped start no longer play as silence.**
- **Changes to files are kept.** In rare cases, the latest change to a file on the player could be lost when the player wrote it back to the disk.
- **The clock reads more reliably.** A slow answer from the clock chip no longer counts as a failure, and a failed read is tried again.
- **Music no longer stops when a screen needs memory.** Opening some screens while music played could stop the song and jump to Now Playing. The memory now comes from a reserve.

## Known issues

- **Very loud, bass-heavy recordings may distort with the equaliser or Stereo Width on.** Some heavily mastered albums can clip inside the audio chip when a preset or a wide stereo setting adds bass. If you hear this, choose a flatter preset or a lower Stereo Width. We are working on a fix.
- **Resuming after a long pause can be slow on hard-drive players.** Pause is now instant, but on a hard-drive player that is building album art, pressing Play again can take several seconds or longer. Flash players are not affected as far as we know.
- **A few names may sort slightly out of place.** When your files carry Sort Artist or Sort Album tags, a handful of entries that differ only by a leading "The" or by spaces at the end can show a little out of order. A fix is planned for 0.9.1.
- **A video's picture can stop for about a second.** Rarely, the picture freezes for about a second while the sound carries on, then catches up. A fix is planned for 0.9.1 or 0.9.2.
- **192 kHz FLAC files are still untested on a real player.** They play at 48 kHz, or at 96 kHz with Hi-Res on, but we have not yet played a 192 kHz album on one.
- **The translations are drafts.** Some German, Dutch and Japanese words may be stiff or wrong.
- **Only one kind of player has been tested on real hardware:** flash, 64 MB, a 3,000 mAh battery and USB-C. Hard-drive players, 32 MB players, the original batteries and accessories on the 30-pin dock connector should work but are untested; reports are very welcome.

## Updating from 0.8.3

Download `ProkBox5g-0.9.0.zip` and follow the update steps in Getting started. Your music, settings, places in books and videos, and playlists are kept. On the first start the player rebuilds its library once; the library page shows how far it has got.
